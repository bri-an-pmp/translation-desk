# Museum and exhibition translation (English → Spanish)

The reader is a museum visitor standing in front of the work. They read a label in under a minute, often with children, often in a second language they grew up with at home. The Spanish must be as clear, accurate and alive as the English, and must fit the same wall space.

## Kinds of text

- **Exhibition title**: often wordplay. Translate the meaning and spirit; when a literal version loses the effect, give your best option in the translation and up to two alternatives in a flag.
- **Section or wall panel (heading + body)**: the curator's voice. Keep their argument, emphasis and tone; don't add explanations or soften claims.
- **Object label (tombstone)**: artist, nationality and dates, title, date, medium, dimensions, credit line, accession number. Follow the line conventions below exactly.
- **Extended label / caption**: short interpretive text next to one object.
- **Credits, acknowledgments and sponsor lines**: names stay as written; translate the roles.

## Artwork titles

Follow the project's title policy. Whatever the policy:

- Keep titles in *italics* exactly where the source has them.
- Titles originally in Spanish, Nahuatl, Portuguese, French or any other language stay exactly as the artist wrote them.
- *Untitled* → *Sin título*.
- Series names follow the same policy as titles.
- When the policy is to translate and you're not sure whether an official Spanish title exists, translate it and flag it so the translator can check the museum's records.

## Wall text style

- Sentences a general visitor can read aloud: prefer one idea per sentence, and split English sentences longer than about 30 words when Spanish would run past 40.
- Active, concrete verbs. Avoid stacked nominalizations (*la realización de la representación de*).
- Use *exponer* for exhibiting work, *obra* for an artist's work, *pieza* for an object, *muestra* or *exposición* for the show (not *exhibición* unless the museum's style says so).
- Place names: use the established Spanish name when there is one (Nueva York, Filadelfia, Misisipi). Keep Indigenous place and people names as the source writes them.
- Institutions: use the official Spanish name only if the institution uses one; otherwise keep the English name.
- Identity terms (Latinx, Latine, Chicano/a/x, Black, Indigenous, queer): follow the source and the museum's style notes. Don't change a self-identification term; flag it if the Spanish choice could be read differently.
- Gender: use the artist's gender when the text makes it clear (*la artista*, *el artista*). When it doesn't, write around it if you can; otherwise flag.

## Length

Spanish runs about 20–30% longer than English. Wall panels and labels are printed in fixed spaces. When a segment has a character limit, the visible text (without italic markers) must fit it; cut words, not meaning, and say in a flag what you cut. When there's no limit, aim for at most about 125% of the English length.

## Faithfulness

- Never add facts, dates or interpretation that the English doesn't contain.
- Never correct the source silently. If a date, name or fact looks wrong, translate it as written and flag it.
- Keep quotations exact. If a quote was originally said in Spanish, flag it: the museum may have the original words, which are better than a back-translation.

## Press releases, media copy and social posts

- **Headings** like MEDIA COPY, CONTACT INFORMATION, SOCIAL MEDIA POST NO. 1 → COMUNICADO DE PRENSA, INFORMACIÓN DE CONTACTO, PUBLICACIÓN PARA REDES SOCIALES N.º 1 (keep the capitalization style of the source).
- **Dates and times**: "March 3 - March 24 2027" → "del 3 al 24 de marzo de 2027"; "1-4 PM" → "1:00 p.m. a 4:00 p.m."; "24 hours / 7 days a week" → "las 24 horas, los 7 días de la semana".
- **Contacts**: emails, URLs, Instagram handles and phone numbers stay exactly as written.
- **Calls to action** to the public use plural usted (Acompáñenos, Escríbanos) unless the register setting says otherwise; a single reader is usted (Si tiene alguna pregunta).
- **Institution names**: keep the English name of US galleries, museums, art schools and foundations (Rhode Island School of Design, Andy Warhol Foundation). Translate names that have an established Spanish form, such as public universities and government bodies: University of Texas at Austin → Universidad de Texas en Austin; City of Houston Office of Cultural Affairs → Oficina de Asuntos Culturales de la Ciudad de Houston.
- **Funding and sponsor lines**: write natural Spanish (Este proyecto cuenta con el apoyo parcial de…), not the passive calque "es apoyado por". If the source already includes a Spanish version of a line, don't copy it blindly: improve it if needed and flag the change.
- **Measurements** stay in the source's units, with the Spanish abbreviation or word: "12 ft x 6 ft" → "12 × 6 pies".
- **Names**: when a Spanish-language name appears without its accent (Ana Lopez), flag it and suggest the accented spelling the person likely uses (Ana López); the translator decides.
- **Typos and errors in the source** (a misspelled word, a misplaced apostrophe such as "the artists' studio" for one artist): translate the intended meaning and flag the error so the client can fix the English too.

## Phrasing that reads as translated, and better choices

| English | Avoid | Prefer |
|---|---|---|
| dilapidated (building) | en ruinas (too strong) | destartalado, deteriorado |
| community-driven | centrado en la comunidad | impulsado por la comunidad, surgido de la comunidad |
| conquest over nature | la conquista … sobre | la conquista de |
| on view | disponible | se podrá visitar, estará abierta al público |
| curated by | está curado por | la curaduría está a cargo de |
| in the physical and temporal register (abstract sense) | en el registro | en los planos, en las dimensiones |
| underground (art scene) | clandestino (suggests illegal) | underground |
| moved back home to (city) | regresó a (city) | regresó a (city), su ciudad natal |

Dangling modifiers in the English ("Drawing on his background…, this project explores") are repaired in Spanish by making the person the subject, and flagged so the client can fix the English.
